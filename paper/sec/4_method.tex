\section{Decision-Time Counterfactual Supervision}
\label{sec:method}

\Cref{sec:probing} suggests what a remedy has to do: not add information, but create, at the states where the task is
decided, pressure to act on the information that is there.
Behavior cloning on fixed layouts never creates this pressure, since the memorized action is always correct.
We therefore supervise the policy in counterfactual worlds built at its own decision states, where the memorized action is
wrong and the correct one is known.

\paragraph{On-policy distillation.}
The student is \pizf with LoRA adapters~\cite{hu2022lora} (rank 32) on the linear layers of the VLM and the action expert;
the teacher is the same checkpoint with the adapters disabled.
Each iteration, the student drives fresh rollouts, and at every queried state $s_t$ the teacher labels the 50-step chunk
$A_t$ (DAgger-style~\cite{ross2011dagger}).
The student is trained with the flow-matching loss of \pizf on these labels, with $\epsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I)$ and the
flow time $\tau\in(0,1]$ drawn from the Beta-shaped distribution of \pizf ($\tau=1$ is pure noise),
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\text{fact}} = \lVert v_\theta(o_t, x_\tau, \tau) - (\epsilon - A_t) \rVert^2,\quad x_\tau = \tau\epsilon + (1-\tau)A_t .
\end{equation}
Without counterfactuals this is our matched control (``Distill''); it leaves \pizf's behavior essentially unchanged.

\paragraph{Counterfactual worlds.}
At a state $s_t$, the simulator renders a world $s'_t$ that differs in one respect, chosen by the phase of the episode:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Retarget} (before the grasp): the target is moved to a free spot, or swaps places with another object; the
hand stays. Label: a privileged scripted approach to above the target's new position, valid for its first $n$ steps (the
grasp is left to the policy).
\item \emph{Co-shift} (before the grasp): hand and target move together, so the hand--target geometry is unchanged and the
teacher's nominal chunk remains correct up to the grasp.
\item \emph{Relocate} (while carrying): the container, or the furniture region the object goes to, is moved; hand and object
stay. Label: a privileged scripted placer to the new location, valid until shortly after the release.
\end{itemize}
The counterfactual observation $o'_t$ and label $A'_t$ (with a validity mask $w$ over chunk steps) enter the same loss with
the same noise and time,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{\text{fact}} + \frac{\sum w \odot \lVert v_\theta(o'_t, x'_\tau, \tau) - (\epsilon - A'_t) \rVert^2}{\sum w},
\end{equation}
on at most 15\% of the states of an iteration.
A pairwise consistency term on $v_\theta(o') - v_\theta(o)$ brought no gain and is not used, in line with~\cite{chen2026ect}.

\paragraph{Keeping the labels valid.}
A scripted teacher with privileged state is only as good as the assumptions behind it, and most of the engineering of the
method went into recognizing where they fail. Three automatic checks, all task-agnostic, decide which pairs are kept:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Uniqueness.} An object (or furniture) with a twin of the same class in the scene is never moved: the
instruction can then only refer to it by where it is (``the bowl next to the plate''), and moving it would change which
object is meant.
\item \emph{Agreement.} In the factual world, the scripted teacher must head where the \pizf teacher heads (cosine at
least 0.5 between their intended displacements over 25 steps). This rejects states where the task starts with another
step, or where the scripted notion of target or container is wrong.
\item \emph{Phase.} Pre-grasp pairs are placed only before the episode's first grasp, and relocate pairs only while an
object of the task is held (gripper closed, object lifted and between the fingers).
\end{itemize}
Objects are recolored at random during the rollouts (with probability 0.5 per episode) to counter a reliance on color that
appears once the policy starts to use the image (\cref{sec:exp:limits}).

\paragraph{Training budget.}
20 iterations of 1024 states each, batch 8, learning rate $5\cdot10^{-5}$ with cosine decay, one suite at a time; every
compared method uses the same teacher, rollouts, budget and optimizer. A run takes 5--7 hours on one H200 GPU, about half
of it simulation and rendering; the counterfactual views add one render per queried state.
Details and the scripted teachers are given in the supplementary material.
