\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

\pizf fails on rearranged scenes not because it cannot see where objects are, but because it does not act on it at the
moments that decide the task.
Separating use from encoding with decision-time interventions makes this visible, explains why adding geometric
information does not help, and points to the remedy: supervision in counterfactual worlds built at those moments, with
labels that are valid there.
The change it induces lives in the VLM's mid-to-late layers rather than in the action pathway.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
Our labels come from privileged scripted teachers in simulation; they cover reaching and placing, not articulated or
contact-rich steps, and their validity checks were refined by per-task failure analysis on the benchmark itself.
Results are for one policy (\pizf), one benchmark family, and per-suite adaptation with a small budget; the multi-step
suites are not improved, and using the scene costs robustness to appearance changes.
The early-approach blind spot, shared by every model we measured, remains open, as does an intervention that targets the
localized layers directly instead of adapting them with data.
